# Planned recurring expenses versus confirmed payment

Original fictional planning example, not an instruction about accounting recognition or tax treatment.

A fictional agency expects monthly rent of Rs 4,500 and an internet charge of Rs 800. The planned total is 4,500 + 800 = 5,300. A repeating plan is not proof that either payment happened. If only rent has been confirmed, the confirmed amount in this exercise is Rs 4,500, with Rs 800 still awaiting payment confirmation.

Suggested tracking columns: description, expected date, planned amount, actual amount if known, payment confirmation and reference. Compare this month's bill with the repeated amount before copying an entry. A changed internet charge should be checked rather than silently carrying forward an outdated plan.

Review overlapping records before adding another expense: an automatically repeated plan and a manually recorded payment could refer to the same bill. The example does not assert how Bolcash implements recurring entries or whether it creates any transaction automatically. Investigate the records before changing them.

The owner approved recurring expenses as a Bolcash feature. These notes do not approve automatic bank payments, guaranteed reminders, automatic bill settlement or a particular schedule setting. A guide may explain the distinction between a recurring expectation and a payment record, then describe the approved feature narrowly. No guarantee of avoiding missed bills or improving cash flow is made.
