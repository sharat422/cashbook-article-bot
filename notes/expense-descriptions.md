# Consistent expense descriptions

Original fictional record-keeping exercise, not accounting classification or tax-deduction advice.

A fictional distributor records Rs 300 for local delivery, Rs 200 for packing tape and Rs 500 for another local delivery. The recorded total is 300 + 200 + 500 = 1,000. The two delivery entries total Rs 800, while the packing-tape entry is Rs 200. These are illustrative grouping choices, not a statutory chart of accounts.

Suggested practice: write a short description of what was purchased, the transaction date and the amount. When reviewing similar expenses, use consistent wording so that transport, delivery and freight are not unknowingly treated as different activities in a manual comparison. Preserve the original transaction detail even if a grouping label is changed.

Check for a duplicated receipt before assuming repeated amounts are separate purchases. A Rs 300 entry may be either a genuine second expense or the same item entered again; matching the amount alone cannot decide. Compare dates and available references before editing.

Bolcash's approved features include recording expenses and viewing reports. No automatic categorisation, specific category list, category-edit workflow or report export format is established here. Present grouping advice as an optional paper/spreadsheet technique rather than a tested app feature. Do not describe an expense as tax deductible or treat grouped expenses as a profit calculation.
