# A balance check before a reminder

Original fictional example, not a real customer story, collection script or legal notice.

A fictional customer has Rs 14,600 recorded as outstanding before a Rs 5,600 payment is confirmed. The new recorded balance is 14,600 - 5,600 = 9,000. Confirm that payment against the business's actual record and the correct customer before quoting the remaining figure.

Suppose the customer also claims a Rs 1,000 return that the distributor has not accepted. Do not silently reduce the recorded balance to Rs 8,000, and do not describe Rs 9,000 as an agreed total. State the payment recorded and the unresolved return separately. Resolve the disagreement before presenting an adjustment as accepted.

Suggested checklist: check customer identity, latest recorded payment, pending confirmations, accepted adjustments, disputed lines and the date of the balance being quoted. A late-arriving payment confirmation can make a previously prepared reminder out of date. Recheck before sending rather than relying on yesterday's total.

An illustrative message can identify the records being queried without pressuring the customer: our records show this payment and this remaining balance; please tell us if a payment or adjustment is missing. Do not use an invented due date, penalty, legal threat or promise of automatic collection. Do not claim Bolcash sends reminders or verifies transfers unless the owner separately approves that feature. These are optional communication practices, not guaranteed collection results.
