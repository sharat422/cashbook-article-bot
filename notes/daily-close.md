# Check a day of customer-ledger movements

Original fictional teaching material. This is an arithmetic and record-review exercise, not a bank reconciliation, cash audit or statutory closing procedure.

A fictional customer's opening ledger balance is Rs 9,500. A new credit entry of Rs 3,600 and a confirmed payment of Rs 4,100 are recorded during the day. The closing ledger balance is 9,500 + 3,600 - 4,100 = 9,000. An opening balance is not another receipt and should not be added again as income.

Suggested review: identify the opening figure, list today's additions and confirmed reductions, inspect their dates and customer identifiers, recalculate and compare with the displayed closing balance. Check whether a similar-looking entry is a separate transaction or a duplicate before changing anything. Keep disputed adjustments visible rather than silently applying them.

A closing ledger balance describes the customer's recorded position. It does not prove how much physical cash is in a drawer, whether a UPI transfer settled or whether a bank statement matches. A count of cash and a check of actual payment records are different activities. No automatic reconciliation or app daily-close feature is approved by these notes.

For a paper or spreadsheet exercise, keep columns for opening balance, credit, confirmed payment, other accepted adjustment and closing balance. Record unresolved questions for the next review. These steps are optional operational suggestions, not a legal requirement or a promise of error-free accounts. Use only owner-approved facts when mentioning Bolcash.
