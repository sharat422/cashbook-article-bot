# Similar names need a record check

Original fictional example. The customer labels are invented and are not real personal data.

A fictional distributor has Customer A, labelled Ramesh Stores, with a recorded balance of Rs 2,500, and Customer B, labelled Ramesh Traders, with Rs 4,000. A confirmed payment of Rs 1,000 belongs to Customer B. The illustrative balances afterwards are Rs 2,500 for A and 4,000 - 1,000 = 3,000 for B. Applying it to A instead would leave the wrong records even though the overall total fell by the same amount.

Suggested check before entering a payment: identify the specific customer record and match it to the actual transaction information. A similar display name is not enough. Use an existing business reference or customer code where available and keep only identifying information needed for the business purpose.

If the wrong record was used, review the original entries and available correction process before changing data. These notes do not establish an app undo, merge or bulk-correction feature. Do not invent a step-by-step Bolcash screen flow.

Bolcash has owner-approved customer records. The guide may mention that feature narrowly while teaching this manual identity check. It must not promise automatic identity matching or duplicate-customer detection. This is arithmetic and operational teaching material, not evidence that a real customer was misidentified or that a particular business outcome is guaranteed.
