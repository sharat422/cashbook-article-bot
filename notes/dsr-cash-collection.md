# Cash collected on the beat but not written down the same day

Original fictional example, not a real salesman or distributor. A fictional DSR visits 18 shops and returns in the evening with cash and a notebook. He hands over Rs 1,42,300. The collection entries made that day add up to Rs 1,36,800. The gap is 1,42,300 - 1,36,800 = Rs 5,500: one collection was never written against a shop.

A month later that retailer says "I paid". With no entry, the distributor either argues or absorbs the amount.

What we do: count each salesman's cash at van return, before the daily close, and match it to the entries by shop. A gap must be explained before the day is closed: no cash entry, no close. Ask the salesman to write each collection in the notebook at the shop, with the shop name and amount.

This is a bookkeeping control, not an accusation of the salesman; an unwritten entry is often forgetfulness. Do not claim Bolcash prevents missing collections or audits salesmen. These are optional practices, not guaranteed outcomes.
