# Original teaching material to review before live use

This is a fictional worked example written for this tutorial. It is not a customer case study, a news report, legal advice or evidence of business results.

An imaginary retailer has an opening amount payable of Rs 18,500. A Rs 7,000 payment is confirmed separately by the distributor. A Rs 1,500 goods-return adjustment is also accepted by the distributor. The illustrative remaining customer balance is calculated as 18,500 - 7,000 - 1,500 = 10,000.

A payment is money received; the goods return in this example is a separate balance adjustment. Keep the dates, the amount, the customer's identity and the reason for each entry. Confirm receipt against the business's actual payment record before marking money as received. If the business disputes the return, do not silently treat the adjustment as accepted.

A proposed daily checklist: review new customer entries; confirm that payments belong to the correct customer; check arithmetic; identify disputed adjustments; keep the remaining amount visible; check the balance before sending a reminder. These are suggested operational steps, not a claim of legal requirements or a guarantee of faster collection.
