1. Retailer pays "on account" without saying which bill
What happens: Retailer sends Rs 15,000 by UPI with no note. We have 3 open bills.
What goes wrong: Staff apply it to the newest bill; the oldest one looks overdue and we send a wrong reminder.
What we do now: Call the same day, ask which bill, write the bill number next to the UPI reference.


2. DSR collects cash on the beat, doesn't write it down the same day
What happens: Salesman collects cash from 15–20 shops, hands over a bag of
cash and a notebook in the evening.
What goes wrong: One collection never got written down. Next month the
retailer says "I paid" and we have no record — we either argue or absorb it.
What we do now: Cash counted per salesman at van return, before the daily
close; no cash entry, no close.


3. Scheme claim rejected because the batch number was never written down
What happens: Retailer returns short-dated stock; we raise the claim with
the brand six weeks later from memory, no batch number, no photo.
What goes wrong: Brand closes the claim — late filing, no batch to trace.
The money (often lakhs across a quarter) stays stuck while we pay interest
on working-capital loans.
What we do now: Open a claim line the day the return is recorded — invoice,
batch, expiry, dated photo together; review open claims every Friday.


4. Salesman gives credit beyond the limit to hit his sales target
What happens: DSR's target is secondary sales, so he keeps billing a slow
payer — more credit, no collection.
What goes wrong: Receivables grow faster than sales. One Coimbatore
distributor's receivables went from Rs 9 lakh to Rs 31 lakh while sales
grew, because two salesmen kept extending credit to slow payers.
What we do now: Credit limit checked at billing; limit reached means
collect first, bill after — salesman can't override it alone.
Source: RetailPOS 2026 DMS guide (Coimbatore distributor story);
Massist CRM ("reps chasing order volume over payment discipline").

5. Retailer disputes the balance because a figure was overwritten
What happens: A wrong entry was scribbled over in the bahi and rewritten.
What goes wrong: Retailer points at the overwritten figure and says the
balance is wrong. We can't prove what it was — the argument costs the
relationship, not just the amount.
What we do now: Never overwrite a figure. One line through it, correct
entry below, and the retailer signs the monthly statement.
