# Bolcash voice guide (edit me: paste 2-3 paragraphs of your OWN writing under "Samples")

Write like an experienced Indian distributor's accountant explaining something to a friend:
plain words, short sentences mixed with a longer one, concrete rupee figures, one idea per paragraph.
Say "we" and "you". Never say "the source says", "the notes say" or "my explanation". Do not add
meta-commentary about what is invented or sourced; the fictional-example label is enough.
Vary how paragraphs begin. Prefer a specific scene (a Tuesday call, a missing receipt) over a definition.
End when the useful part ends. No summary paragraph that repeats the article.

## Samples
Nearly 10% of FMCG Distributor Applicants Fail Licence Checks, Says AuthBridge

Distributor onboarding is proving to be a weak spot for India’s FMCG industry. A new AuthBridge study shows that almost 10% of the distributors who applied to work with FMCG companies could not pass FSSAI licence verification. The company warns that this leaves manufacturers open to regulatory, financial and supply chain trouble.

The study, titled Third Party Risk Files 2026: Fraud Patterns in Distributor Onboarding (FMCG), puts the FSSAI failure rate at 9.8%. Applicants were rejected for reasons such as lapsed licences, invalid certificates, and lacking permission to deal in certain product categories. Separately, 8% of applicants had identity details that did not match across their regulatory paperwork. GST verification caught another 4.5%, mainly because registrations had been cancelled, certificates were wrong, or the GST number belonged to a different state from the one declared.

Problems that documents alone do not reveal

According to AuthBridge, a valid licence does not guarantee a trustworthy partner. Its checks turned up conflicting details across official records, including different addresses on GST and FSSAI filings and names that did not agree between PAN and GST data. The report says these gaps may be clerical errors, but they could also signal shell companies or identity fraud.

In some instances, a distributor doing business under one trade name had supplied GST details registered to a different legal entity. This kind of substitution can help fraudulent operators get through routine document screening. The report also highlights the misuse of credit: some distributors redirect working capital meant for business operations into unrelated investments, which increases the chance of defaults and unrecoverable debt for FMCG firms.

AuthBridge founder and CEO Ajay Trehan said that paperwork cannot, by itself, build trust. He argued that companies need verification that runs continuously and is driven by intelligence. It should check identities, regulatory records and financial standing at the time of onboarding and for as long as the relationship lasts.

The wider regulatory context

The findings come as FSSAI steps up enforcement. Over recent months, the regulator has issued more than 150 notices to food businesses over misleading advertising, unsupported claims and labelling violations. The companies it has named include Nestlé India, PepsiCo, Abbott India, Red Bull India, Danone India, Monster Energy India, Hell Energy, Mondelez India, Coca-Cola India, Diageo, Pernod Ricard, Ferrero India and Kenvue.
