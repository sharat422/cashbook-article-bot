# How to record a partial customer payment without losing track of the balance

This is a fictional training example. It demonstrates the article workflow and is not intended for publication as reporting or a customer success story.

An imaginary retailer owes a distributor Rs 18,500. The distributor confirms a payment of Rs 7,000 and accepts a separate goods-return adjustment of Rs 1,500. The remaining balance is Rs 10,000: 18,500 - 7,000 - 1,500 = 10,000. [demo_source]

## Keep the two changes separate

The payment represents money received. The goods return represents an adjustment accepted by the distributor. Recording both as one payment would hide why the original amount changed. In this example, keep a payment entry and a separate note explaining the accepted adjustment. [demo_source]

Before marking a payment as received, check the business's actual payment record and confirm which customer it belongs to. A message about a transfer is something to investigate; the example assumes receipt has already been confirmed.

## A small review checklist

First, check the customer's identity and the opening amount. Then confirm the amount received and its date. Record the separate reason for the accepted return adjustment. Finally, recalculate the remaining balance and check it before sending a reminder. These are proposed operational steps, not legal requirements or a promise of business results. [demo_source]

If the return is disputed, pause and resolve that disagreement instead of silently treating the adjustment as accepted. Clear entries help the owner explain the example's arithmetic later.
